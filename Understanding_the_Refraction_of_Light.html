Light enters a more dense medium (glass), so it slows down and bends towards the normal. A 45° incident ray refracts to ~28°–30° (smaller than 45°).
f = 180 mm
How a Convex (Converging) Lens works: Parallel light rays bend inwards twice (entering and leaving the curved glass) so they converge at a single focal point (F) to form a focused image.
3 Uses of Converging Lenses
  • Magnifying glass — enlarges nearby text or specimens
  • Camera lens — focuses light onto the camera sensor
  • Human eye & reading glasses — focuses light onto the retina
Analogy 1

Car & Mud Patch

Hitting mud at 90° (along the normal) slows both front wheels together — the car goes straight. Hitting at an angle slows one wheel first, turning the car towards the normal.

Real-Life 2

Bent Pencil in Water

Light from the submerged pencil leaves denser water into less-dense air, speeding up and bending away from the normal. Your brain traces straight lines back, making it look bent.

UAE Context 3

Desert Summer Mirages

Hot air near summer sand is less dense than cooler air above. Sky light refracts gradually through these changing air densities, bending upward so it mimics water on the ground.

Dispersion: White light is a mixture of 7 colours (Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet). Because refraction depends on wavelength, violet slows down most in glass and refracts more sharply than red, separating the beam into a visible spectrum.
Toggle Primary Light Beams (Cone Cells)
Resulting Overlap Colour
White (Red + Green + Blue)
Light (RGB) vs Paint (RYB): Digital screens and spotlights mix Red, Green, and Blue light — matching the 3 types of cone cells in the human eye. Combining two produces secondary light colours: Yellow (R+G), Cyan (G+B), and Magenta (R+B). Art paints use RYB pigments instead.
Choose Optical Filter (with White Torch)
Part-by-Part Appearance
A red optical filter transmits red wavelengths and absorbs all others. Regions that cannot reflect red light (like the green jacket and blue jeans) appear black.
